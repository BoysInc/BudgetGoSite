Holiday Tipping Guide: Who to Tip and How Much (With the Math)

Holiday tips are a thank-you, not a bill. Here's how to decide who gets one and keep the total in your budget.

Key takeaways

  • Tip the people who help you most often all year, and set a total before you pick amounts.
  • Common rules of thumb: about one visit's cost for regular services, up to a week's pay for a cleaner or nanny, and a small gift for teachers.
  • Mail carriers can't accept cash. A non-cash gift of $20 or less is allowed.

Every November, people start searching for a holiday tipping guide, and most guides hand you a long list of people with a dollar range next to each. That's a good start, but it skips the question that matters: what can you actually afford? This holiday tipping guide covers who to tip and how much, then adds it all up in a worked example so the thank-yous fit inside your December budget.

Who to tip for the holidays

A holiday tip says thanks to someone who makes your life easier through the year. The people who usually make the list:

  • Regular help at home: a house cleaner, nanny, babysitter or dog walker
  • Personal care: a hairstylist, barber or nail tech you see often
  • Your kids' people: teachers, daycare staff and coaches
  • Neighborhood services: the mail carrier, trash and recycling crew, a landscaper or snow remover, and building staff like doormen

You don't need to tip everyone. Start with how often you see someone and how much they help. Someone who cleans your home every two weeks ranks well above a service you use twice a year.

What people actually give

For a reality check, Bankrate's 2025 holiday tipping survey of 2,445 US adults found the median planned tip was $50 for a housekeeper, $48 for a childcare provider, $50 for a landscaper or snow remover, $25 for a teacher, $25 for trash and recycling collectors and $20 for a mail carrier (Bankrate, December 2025).

The same survey found that fewer people planned to tip than the year before, and 38% said they often feel obligated to tip no matter the service. So if you're trimming your list this year, you're in good company.

How much to tip your mail carrier for Christmas

This one has real rules. US Postal Service carriers may accept a gift worth $20 or less per occasion, such as Christmas, and no more than $50 in gifts from one customer in a year. They can't accept cash or anything that works like cash, such as checks or prepaid Visa or Mastercard gift cards (USPS Postal Bulletin).

A good fit: a $10 to $20 gift card to a local coffee shop, a box of treats, or a card with a thank-you note. Private delivery companies set their own policies, so a small snack basket by the door is a safe default for package drivers.

How much to tip your housekeeper or cleaner for the holidays

The common rule of thumb is up to one week's pay, or the cost of one visit if they come every week or two. If your cleaner charges $140 a visit, a $140 tip is generous and normal. If that's too much this year, half a visit plus a handwritten note is still a real thank-you.

The same idea works for a nanny (often a week's pay, sometimes more for full-time care), a regular babysitter (about one evening's pay) and a dog walker (about a week of walks).

Holiday tips for teachers, hairstylists and everyone else

  • Teachers: many schools discourage cash, so a small gift card or a class gift that several parents chip into is common. Check your school's policy first.
  • Hairstylist, barber or nail tech: about the cost of one service, or simply a bigger tip at your December appointment.
  • Trash and recycling crew: a small cash tip or gift per person if your city allows it. Some public employers ban gifts, so check your local rules.
  • Doormen and building staff: amounts vary a lot by city and building. Ask a neighbor what's typical where you live.

A worked example: one household's tipping budget (with the math)

Say you want to thank six people this year. Here's the list using the rules of thumb above:

Example holiday tipping budget by person
WhoRule of thumbAmount
House cleaner (every 2 weeks, $140 a visit)One visit$140
Dog walker (3 walks a week, $20 each)One week$60
Hairstylist ($65 cut)One service$65
Child's teacherGift card$25
Mail carrierNon-cash gift, $20 max$20
Trash crew (2 people)$15 each$30

Total: $140 + $60 + $65 + $25 + $20 + $30 = $340.

Now check it against your money, not the etiquette list. If you take home $4,000 a month and your 50/30/20 split leaves $1,200 for wants, $340 is more than a quarter of December's wants, on top of gifts, food and travel. Two easy fixes:

  • Spread it out. Set aside $170 in November and $170 in December, so it doesn't land all at once.
  • Trim the biggest line. Give the cleaner half a visit ($70) with a note, and the total drops to $340 − $70 = $270.

Either way, decide the total first. Tips get much bigger when you decide them one doorstep at a time.

How to handle holiday tipping on a tight budget

If money is tight, a thank-you doesn't have to be cash. Ideas that still land well:

  • Prioritize. Tip the one or two people you rely on most and send cards to everyone else.
  • Give time or food. Homemade cookies or a small treat bag goes a long way, especially for mail carriers and drivers.
  • Write it down. A specific note ("thanks for always leaving packages out of the rain") is remembered longer than $10.
  • Leave a review. For a cleaner, stylist or dog walker who runs their own business, a public review can be worth more than a tip.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude not to tip for the holidays?

No. Holiday tips are optional thank-yous, not fees. If you can't afford one, a sincere card or small homemade gift is a kind and normal choice.

Can you give your mail carrier cash or a Visa gift card?

No. USPS carriers can't accept cash or cash-like gift cards in any amount. A store-specific gift card or gift worth $20 or less per occasion is allowed.

Should I give a holiday tip if I already tip every visit?

It's up to you. If you tip well at every appointment, a card or a slightly larger tip at your last visit of the year is plenty. Holiday tips matter most for people you don't tip during the year, like a cleaner on a flat rate.

When should you give holiday tips?

Early to mid-December, at the last regular visit before the holidays. That way people get the money before their own holiday spending, and you can plan the total into one month.
