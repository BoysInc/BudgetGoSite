How Much Should You Spend on Eating Out? (With the Math)

The national average is a starting point. Your last three months of statements are the real answer.

Key takeaways

  • Americans spent about $329 a month per person on eating out in 2024, but a budget should follow your goals, not the average.
  • Start from what you actually spent over the last three months, then trim it by an amount you choose.
  • Turn the monthly number into a per-meal limit so you can decide in the moment, and check your pace weekly.

How much to spend on eating out is a hard number to pin down, because it hides inside lunches, coffees, delivery and the odd dinner with friends. This guide shows you how to set how much to spend on eating out per month from your own spending, with a full example, a per-meal limit and a simple way to track it.

What the average person spends on eating out

According to Ramsey Solutions, using US Bureau of Labor Statistics data, Americans spent an average of $3,945 on eating out in 2024, about $329 a month. By household, the same article lists roughly $218 a month for a single person, $372 for a married couple with no kids and $498 for a married couple with kids (Ramsey Solutions).

Use these as a check, not a target. Where you live, how far you commute and how often you host all change the right number for you.

How much should you budget for eating out per month?

Ramsey suggests lower amounts than the average, tied to your goals. For a family, that looks like $0 to $150 while paying off debt, $100 to $250 while building an emergency fund, and $250 to $400 once finances are stable (Ramsey Solutions).

That's a useful way to think about it. Eating out is a "want", so it should flex with what else you're trying to do. If you follow the 50/30/20 rule, it comes out of your wants bucket, alongside entertainment and shopping.

Step 1: Find out what you spend now

Pull your last three months of transactions and add up everything at restaurants, cafés, takeaway and delivery apps. Don't forget small items. Three months smooths out the month with a birthday and the month with a work trip. Our guide to tracking spending without a spreadsheet shows how to get everything in one place.

Step 2: Choose a number you can stick to

Take the three-month average and decide how much to trim. A cut of 20 to 30% is easier to keep than going to zero. Then check it against your goal: if you're trying to build savings, send the difference straight to savings so the cut doesn't just get spent elsewhere.

A worked example (with the math)

Say you take home $4,000 a month. Your last three months of eating out were $520, $610 and $570.

  • Three-month average: ($520 + $610 + $570) ÷ 3 = $567, about $570
  • You want to move $150 a month to savings
  • New eating-out budget: $570 − $150 = $420, which is 10.5% of take-home pay

Now split the $420 into meals you can plan:

That adds up to exactly $420. The buffer is on purpose: there's always a surprise birthday or a late night at the office.

How to cut back on eating out without feeling deprived

  • Plan the good ones. Pick the dinners you'll really enjoy and say no to the forgettable ones.
  • Shift the meal. Lunch menus are usually cheaper than dinner menus for the same restaurant.
  • Cap delivery. Fees and tips make a delivered meal cost more than the menu price. Give delivery its own line so you see it.
  • Eat first, then shop. Many "I'll just grab something" meals start with an empty stomach and an empty fridge. If groceries are part of the problem, see how much to spend on groceries.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on eating out per month?

Start from your last three months of spending, then trim it to fit your goals. For reference, the 2024 US average was about $329 per person a month, and Ramsey Solutions suggests lower family amounts when you're paying off debt or building savings.

What percentage of income should go to eating out?

There's no official rule, and Ramsey Solutions doesn't give a percentage either. Your goals matter more than any percentage. In the example above, $420 on $4,000 is 10.5%.

Does coffee and delivery count as eating out?

Yes. Anything you buy ready to eat or drink outside your grocery shop belongs in the same total, otherwise the small purchases hide the real number.

How do I stick to my eating-out budget?

Turn the monthly number into a per-meal limit, plan your favourite dinners, and check your total once a week. Seeing the number as you spend is what changes behavior.
