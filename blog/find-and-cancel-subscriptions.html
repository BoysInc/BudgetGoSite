How to Find and Cancel Subscriptions You Forgot About

Free trials, yearly renewals, and apps you opened once. A 20-minute audit can find money you didn't know you were spending.

Why subscriptions are easy to forget

Subscriptions are designed to fade into the background. The charge is small, it repeats automatically, and it often comes from a card you don't look at closely. Yearly plans are even easier to miss because they only show up once.

Step 1: List every recurring charge

Go through the last three months of bank and card statements. Look for the same merchant and a similar amount appearing on a regular schedule. Don't forget:

  • Streaming, music, and cloud storage
  • Gym and fitness apps
  • Software and news subscriptions
  • Free trials that quietly turned into paid plans

Step 2: Check your App Store subscriptions

Subscriptions you bought through the App Store are listed in one place: open Settings, tap your name, then tap Subscriptions. You can cancel any of them there. Subscriptions you signed up for on a website need to be cancelled on that website.

Step 3: Keep, cancel, or downgrade

For each subscription, ask one question: did I use this in the last 30 days?

  • Yes, often: keep it. Consider switching to a yearly plan if it's cheaper.
  • Sometimes: downgrade to a cheaper tier, or share a family plan.
  • No: cancel it. You can always sign up again later.

Rotating helps too: keep one streaming service at a time and switch when you've finished what you wanted to watch.

Step 4: Stop surprise renewals

When you start a free trial, cancel straight away if the service allows you to keep access until the trial ends. For yearly plans, put a reminder a week before the renewal date. Then keep an eye on upcoming charges each week so nothing slips through.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I audit my subscriptions?

A full audit every few months is enough if you check upcoming charges weekly.

Will cancelling end access immediately?

Usually not. Most subscriptions, including App Store ones, keep working until the end of the period you already paid for.
