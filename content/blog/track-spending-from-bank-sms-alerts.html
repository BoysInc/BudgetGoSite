Key takeaways

  • An Apple Shortcuts Message automation can pass each bank alert to BudgetGo, which turns it into a transaction with an amount, merchant and category.
  • Limit the automation to your bank's sender or a word every alert uses, so personal texts never leave Messages.
  • BudgetGo reads the alert on your device and only creates a transaction when it recognizes a financial message.

If your bank texts you every time your card is used, you already have a running log of your spending. It's just stuck in your messages. The easiest way to track spending from bank SMS alerts is a Shortcuts automation that hands each alert to your spending tracker the moment it arrives. This guide shows how to set it up in BudgetGo, how to keep it limited to your bank, and what to check when an alert doesn't turn into a transaction.

Why bank alerts make a good spending tracker

A typical alert reads something like “Your card ending 4821 was used for $45.00 at Talabat.” That one line holds most of what a spending tracker needs: how much you spent and where. And because the bank sends it within moments of the payment, it covers spending that other automatic sources can miss:

  • Physical card payments. Swiping or inserting your card doesn't go through Apple Wallet, but the bank still texts you.
  • Online and in-app purchases. Many banks alert you for these too.
  • Transfers and debits. Some banks text you when money leaves your account by transfer, not just by card.
  • Banks that don't connect. If your bank isn't supported for linking, its texts can still reach your tracker.

The catch is that apps can't read your texts on their own. Apple's Developer Technical Support has confirmed that third-party apps can't read your Messages conversations. A Shortcuts automation is the supported way around that: you decide which messages get passed along, and the app only sees those.

How SMS tracking works in BudgetGo

Apple's Shortcuts app has a personal automation trigger called Message. It runs when a text arrives that matches a filter you set, such as a specific sender or a word in the message. Since iOS 17, Message automations can be set to run immediately, without waiting for you to tap a confirmation.

BudgetGo adds a Shortcuts action called Import SMS Transaction. Connect the two once, and each bank alert follows the same path:

  1. Your bank texts you about a payment.
  2. Shortcuts sees that the text matches your filter and runs the automation.
  3. BudgetGo reads the message on your device, logs the amount and merchant, and AI gives it a category.

If a message isn't a recognized financial alert, such as a one-time passcode or a promotion, no transaction is created.

What you need before you start

Run through these first and the setup should work on the first try:

  • Your bank sends alerts by text. Check your bank's app or website for alert settings, and turn on card payment alerts if they're off. Alerts that arrive by email, in a banking app or through a chat app won't trigger a Message automation.
  • BudgetGo is installed and you've opened it once. Opening the app after installing or updating registers its actions with Shortcuts.
  • SMS imports are on. In BudgetGo, open Data Hub, tap SMS Automation and switch on SMS forwarding. When it's off, BudgetGo ignores anything the automation sends.

How to set up the bank SMS automation, step by step

Prefer to watch? Our bank SMS setup video shows every tap in 1 minute 58 seconds. Here are the same steps in writing:

  1. Create a Message automation. Open Shortcuts, go to the Automation tab, tap + and choose Message.
  2. Limit it to your bank. Tap Sender and choose your bank's contact or number. Or use Message Contains with a word that appears in every alert, such as your bank's name or a word like “debited”.
  3. Choose Run Immediately, then tap Next.
  4. Add the BudgetGo action. Tap New Blank Automation, search for BudgetGo and choose Import SMS Transaction.
  5. Pass in the message. Tap the Message field, choose Select Variable, pick Shortcut Input and set it to Content.
  6. Add the sender (optional). Tap the Sender field and choose Shortcut Input set to Sender. It helps BudgetGo identify your bank. Tap Done.
  7. Test it. Wait for your next bank alert, or open SMS Automation in BudgetGo's Data Hub and tap Test connection. Then check the imported transaction in BudgetGo.

If you get alerts from more than one bank, create one automation per bank, each with its own filter.

Keep the filter narrow to protect your privacy

The filter is what decides which texts reach BudgetGo, so it's worth getting right. A filter that only matches your bank keeps conversations with friends and family inside Messages, where they belong.

  • Sender is the tightest option. If your bank always texts from the same number or short code, filter on that.
  • Pick a specific word for Message Contains. Your bank's name, or a phrase only its alerts use, works better than a common word like “paid”, which friends might text you too.
  • Check a few real alerts first. Scroll back through your bank's messages and pick a word that appears in every payment alert.

On BudgetGo's side, alerts are parsed on your device, PII filters limit sensitive details during AI analysis, and BudgetGo does not sell your personal information. You can review, edit or delete any transaction it creates.

Bank SMS automation not working? Quick fixes

  • Can't find the BudgetGo action in Shortcuts. Open BudgetGo once so iOS registers its actions, then search Shortcuts again.
  • The automation asks before it runs. Edit it in Shortcuts and switch from Run After Confirmation to Run Immediately.
  • An alert arrived but no transaction appeared. Make sure SMS forwarding is on in Data Hub and that the Message field uses Shortcut Input set to Content. Only supported financial messages create transactions, so a text that isn't a payment alert won't be imported.
  • The automation never runs. Your filter may not match. Compare it with a recent alert: the sender may have changed, or the word you chose isn't in every message.

Combine SMS with your other sources

Bank alerts are one piece of the picture. Cash never triggers a text, and not every bank alerts you for every payment. To see all your spending in one place, pair SMS tracking with the sources that fit how you pay:

  • Apple Pay. A Wallet automation can track Apple Pay spending automatically the moment you tap.
  • Connected banks. Link one or more banks through Plaid, where supported.
  • Voice and manual entry. Say what you spent in cash, then review it before saving.
  • Imports. Bring in PDF bank statements, CSV or Excel exports, or receipt photos for older history.

If one card reaches BudgetGo through two sources, for example an Apple Pay tap that also triggers a bank text, glance at your transactions in the first week to make sure each payment appears once. Then pick the source you trust most for that card.

Frequently asked questions

Can a budgeting app read my text messages?

Not on its own. Third-party apps can't read your Messages conversations. With a Shortcuts automation, only the texts that match your filter are passed to BudgetGo.

Which banks work with SMS tracking?

Any bank that texts you about payments can trigger the automation. BudgetGo creates a transaction only when it recognizes the message as a financial alert, so check your first few imports after setup.

Do I need to link my bank to use SMS alerts?

No. SMS tracking works without a bank login. Linking a bank through Plaid is optional and can cover payments your bank doesn't text you about.

Do I need a budget to track spending from SMS alerts?

No. BudgetGo works in tracking-only mode with no budget, or with one active budget at a time. Alerts are logged and categorized either way.
