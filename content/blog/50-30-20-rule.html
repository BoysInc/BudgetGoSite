Key takeaways

  • Split take-home pay: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and debt payoff.
  • It's a starting point. If rent is high, 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 is fine.
  • The rule only works if you can see where your money actually goes.

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting method that divides your after-tax income into three buckets. It was popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in their book All Your Worth. Instead of tracking dozens of line items, you only need to keep three numbers in balance.

  • 50% for needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport to work, and minimum debt payments.
  • 30% for wants: dining out, streaming, shopping, travel, hobbies, and anything you could live without.
  • 20% for savings: emergency fund, retirement, investing, and extra debt payments above the minimum.

A 50/30/20 budget example

Say you take home $4,000 a month after taxes. Here's how the split looks:

50/30/20 split for $4,000 monthly take-home pay
BucketShareMonthly amount
Needs50%$2,000
Wants30%$1,200
Savings & debt payoff20%$800

That means you can spend up to $3,200 a month on needs and wants combined, and $800 goes somewhere it keeps working for you.

Needs vs. wants: where people get stuck

The hardest part is being honest about what counts as a need. A phone plan is a need; the newest phone on a monthly plan is partly a want. Groceries are a need; food delivery is a want. A quick test: if you skipped it for a month, would anything bad actually happen? If not, it's a want.

When the 50/30/20 rule doesn't fit

In high-cost cities, housing alone can eat half your income. That doesn't mean budgeting failed. Adjust the ratio and keep the idea:

  • 60/20/20 when needs run high but you still want to protect savings.
  • 70/20/10 as a temporary plan while you pay down debt or move to cheaper rent.
  • 40/30/30 if your fixed costs are low and you want to save aggressively.

The goal is the same: decide your split on purpose instead of finding out at the end of the month.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 50/30/20 rule based on gross or net income?

Net income: what lands in your account after taxes. If retirement contributions come out of your paycheck automatically, you can count them toward your 20%.

Does debt go in needs or savings?

Minimum payments are needs. Anything extra you pay to clear debt faster counts toward the 20%.
