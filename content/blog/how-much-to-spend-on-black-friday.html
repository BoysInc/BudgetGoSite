Key takeaways

  • Your Black Friday budget is a slice of your holiday budget, not a separate pot.
  • Write a ranked list first, then add a small, fixed cap for deals you didn't plan.
  • A discount only counts as saving if you were going to buy the item anyway.

Black Friday lands on November 27 this year, with Cyber Monday on November 30, and the deal emails have already started. If you're wondering how much to spend on Black Friday, the honest answer is: what is left in your holiday budget after the essentials, and not a dollar more. This guide shows you how to find that number, with a worked example, and how to keep an eye on it while you shop.

Why you need a Black Friday budget before the sales start

Sales are built to make "yes" feel urgent. In an October 2025 survey of 1,009 Australian adults, CHOICE found that 53% of people who bought in a Black Friday sale regretted a purchase or had a bad experience. Of those shoppers, 52% said they spent more than they intended and 48% said they bought things they didn't need. It's a non-US survey, but the pattern is familiar anywhere: no limit, no plan, easy overspend.

A number decided in advance turns each deal into a simple question: does it fit, or doesn't it?

Step 1: Find your Black Friday number

Start with the money you've set aside for the season, not with the deals. If you've already made your Christmas gift budget, Black Friday is where part of that money gets spent, so it comes out of the same total. Then:

  • Take out what isn't a sale item: food, travel, hosting and events.
  • Subtract what you've already bought for gifts or for yourself this season.
  • Check it against your month. If you follow the 50/30/20 rule, the money should come from your "wants" bucket, never from rent, bills or savings.

What's left is your ceiling. For context only, a Self Financial survey reported by The Motley Fool put the typical expected Black Friday spend near $400 (The Motley Fool). Treat any average as a sanity check, not a target.

Step 2: Build a ranked list, then add a small impulse cap

List what you actually plan to buy and rank it: needs first, then gifts, then wants. Put a realistic price next to each. Then set aside a fixed impulse cap, around 20% of the ceiling, for the deal you didn't see coming. When that cap is gone, it's gone.

A worked example (with the math)

Say your holiday budget is $800. Hosting and a work party take $200, and you've already spent $300 on gifts. That leaves $300 for Black Friday weekend.

Headphones for your partner$90Gift
Board game for the kids$35Gift
Gift for a parent$30Gift
Impulse cap (20% of $300)$60Unplanned

The planned items add up to $240, and $240 + $60 = $300. If the coat usually costs $120 and you get it for $85, you saved $35, because it was on your list anyway. If you pick up a $60 gadget nobody asked for, you spent $60, even if it was "40% off."

How to avoid overspending on Black Friday

  • Unsubscribe or mute promo emails until you've made your list.
  • Ask "would I buy this at full price?" If not, skip it.
  • Wait 24 hours on anything unplanned. Real deals often last through Cyber Monday.
  • Avoid new debt. If a purchase only works on credit, it isn't in the budget.
  • Check subscriptions. Free-trial offers tied to deals can turn into new monthly charges. A quick subscription audit catches them.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on Black Friday?

Whatever is left in your holiday budget after food, travel and events, and after what you've already bought. Add a small impulse cap, and don't borrow to hit a deal.

Is Black Friday worth it?

It is when you buy things already on your list at a lower price. It isn't when the discount is the only reason you're buying.

Should I shop on Black Friday or Cyber Monday?

Either works. Decide from your list. Many online deals run across the whole weekend, so you rarely need to rush.

What if I overspend?

Return what you can, then move the overage into next month's budget as a clear line so it gets paid off rather than forgotten.
